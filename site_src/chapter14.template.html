Part 1the appendix

What is in the appendix

The book covers thirteen patterns in depth. The Gang of Four catalog has 23. These are the remaining nine — less common, not less useful, and documented here with just enough detail to recognise when one applies.

How to read an appendix entry

  • A scenario. Every pattern here starts with a concrete problem — a TV remote, a theme-park planner, a tree-filled landscape, a monster game.
  • The intent, in one line. “Use the X Pattern when…” — the sentence you should be able to recall before reaching for it.
  • Uses and drawbacks. Every one of these nine ships with a real cost, and the book names it: Bridge increases complexity, Flyweight removes independence, Visitor breaks encapsulation, Memento can be slow.
  • No demo code. Unlike the main chapters, the appendix deliberately stays at the level of “here is the shape of the solution”.
Why they are here and not in a chapter: they are rarer, narrower, or only worth their complexity in specific situations. That is a judgement about frequency, not quality — a Bridge in a graphics library is not a compromise.

Part 3chain, interpreter, mediator, memento, visitor

Patterns about who does the work

Five patterns about the shape of control: passing a request along a chain, interpreting a tiny language, centralising a mesh of conversations, saving state you are not supposed to touch, and adding operations to a structure you cannot change.

Read the drawbacks column. Every appendix pattern ships with one, and they are the reason these nine are not in chapters: Bridge adds indirection, Flyweight removes per-instance behaviour, Visitor breaks encapsulation, Mediator can become a god object, and a chain can silently drop a request.

What the appendix teaches about patterns

  • Frequency is not quality. These nine are official GoF patterns; they are simply less often the right answer.
  • Every pattern has a cost. The appendix prints uses and drawbacks side by side, on every page.
  • Patterns compose with what you know. Visitor traverses a Composite; Memento often pairs with Command for undo; Bridge is what Factory Method grows into.
  • Recognition beats recall. You are not expected to memorise nine diagrams — only to recognise the scenario when it appears.
  • Twenty-two patterns and one compound pattern later, the vocabulary is the deliverable.
